Do embassies follow the money?

186 countries, embassies sent abroad vs. GDP (2023). The gray line is the actual global pattern — a power-law fit (R² = 0.81). Countries are colored only when they land far off that line.

1. Embassies sent vs. the size of the economy

Both axes are log scale, so the fit line is straight. Above the line = more embassies than GDP predicts. Below = fewer.

2. Embassies sent vs. embassies hosted

Log scale, both axes. The dashed diagonal is where sent = hosted. Below it: sends more than it receives. Above it: hosts more than it sends.

Chart 1 excludes Bermuda (a territory, not a sovereign state). Chart 2 excludes "Northern Cyprus" — the source data mislabels ~28 ordinary embassies in Nicosia, Cyprus as being hosted there; in reality only Turkey recognizes and maintains an embassy in Northern Cyprus. Kosovo's hosted count (1) is likely undercounted by the source (Wikidata) — in reality roughly 20–30 countries maintain a resident mission in Pristina — but the direction (sends far more than it receives) matches independent reporting. Sovereign Military Order of Malta and Palestine are not UN member states but run active networks of missions abroad. Source: Wikidata "database of embassies"; World Bank GDP (NY.GDP.MKTP.CD, 2023).
